## Problem and root cause

A 3840x2160, 29.97 fps video was smooth in a large window but dropped roughly
25% of decoded revisions in the original small window. The distinction was
viewport size, not the window-manager maximize flag. Native CPU scene work was
short; GPU fence completion and frame completion had long tails.

At a 1538x866 physical output the quality resampler selects a 1920x1080 first
pyramid level. That level read raw video planes, repeating plane reads, color
conversion and transfer-function decoding inside the filter's sampling loop.
The existing source-sized RGBA32F conversion was restricted to zero pyramid
levels. Larger outputs already reused it.

Enabling admission for the first reduction exposed another bug: a retained,
culled pass did not necessarily receive updatePaintNode after it stopped being
the active consumer. Its reservation could block the newly selected consumer
under the unchanged 144 MiB admission budget. A 4K resize regression reproduced
this failed handoff before the native fix.

The preceding diagnostic report is
`profiler/reports/profile-report-f4-windowed-2026-10-08-002658.md`.

## Solution

- Select one active raw-video consumer: the final effect for a direct path, or
  the first reduction for a pyramid. Only that consumer requests admission.
- Keep one shared, source-sized conversion Loader. The first reduction reads
  its full-precision linear texels; subsequent RGBA8 levels remain unchanged.
  Use the source's displayed/cropped/rotated pixel dimensions, not a reduced
  level's admitted dimensions, for the conversion resource.
- Retain existing source limits, aggregate/per-layer admission budget, native
  capability checks, failure latch and direct-YUV fallback. Native/nearest,
  reference and hardware-motion sampling remain excluded. No float16,
  encoded-color, bilinear-playback or reduced-precision substitute was added.
- Release an inactive node's reservation before scene synchronization, even
  when that node is culled. The connection has the node as its lifetime context
  and guards the effect/node with QPointer. It does not schedule idle updates.
  The GUI is blocked during this render-thread phase, as documented for
  [QQuickWindow::beforeSynchronizing](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qquickwindow.html#beforeSynchronizing).
- Guard both Loader.Ready and its non-null item before accessing its texture.
- Extend opt-in F4_VIEWER_RESAMPLE_DEBUG diagnostics with consumer/size and
  `[FIX:viewer-pyramid]` inactive-admission release. No default per-frame logging
  was introduced by this fix.

Filter weights, odd-axis sampling extents, color math, alpha, RGBA8 pyramid
quantization, dithering, presentation caching and resting geometry are unchanged.
The admission budget is not a claim that total transient GPU allocations,
including resources retained for in-flight frames, never exceed that amount.

Regression first: a paused NV12 pyramid at DPR 1.75 produced zero conversions
instead of one in `regression-before.txt`. The new 4K direct/pyramid resize
check subsequently reproduced denied admission during a consumer handoff.
Both checks pass after their respective fixes.

The hardware render-control fixtures check:

- Exact rendered output (comparison tolerance zero) against reference sampling
  for direct, half, quarter and odd-sized pyramids at DPR 1 and 1.75, with fresh
  revisions, rotations and mirroring.

## Prevention

Test the quality pyramid as well as the direct path whenever optimizing video
source work. Assert actual converted revisions and rendered equivalence, not
just a requested cache property. Exercise both sides of level boundaries with
a near-budget 4K source: small synthetic textures do not expose double-admission
failures. Retained invisible nodes need lifecycle accounting outside paint-node
updates. Keep pause/idle, failure fallback and 175% scene-transform checks beside
the performance cases; do not lower quality or raise memory budgets to mask lag.
